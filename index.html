Abstract

A disciplined study of predictive signal, not a manufactured success story.

This study investigates whether a leakage-controlled set of content, search-demand, competition, freshness, and content-production signals can explain observed content momentum and support a transparent prioritization framework for human content review.


The analysis deliberately separates predictive modeling from exploratory opportunity ranking. Identifiers are excluded from the feature matrix, target-derived variables are removed, missing-value treatment is learned inside pipelines, and grouped train/test validation is used as the primary estimate because observations belong to anonymized clients.


The resulting evidence does not support a claim that the tested machine-learning models provide generalizable predictive improvement over the mean baseline under grouped held-out validation. The operational contribution is therefore framed as an auditable decision-support layer rather than an autonomous forecasting system.

Research Design

Methodological safeguards were treated as first-class research objects.

The capstone is intentionally structured around validity, reproducibility, interpretability, and disciplined inference.

01

Target Audit

The target variable is audited before modeling to establish what is actually being measured.

02

Leakage Control

Variables that directly encode or temporally entangle the target are excluded.

03

Grouped Validation

Client groups are separated between training and testing to prevent optimistic leakage across related observations.

04

Baseline Comparison

A mean dummy regressor is mandatory, preventing weak models from being presented as useful merely because they are technically non-trivial.

05

Pipeline Integrity

Missing-value treatment and preprocessing are learned inside modeling pipelines after the split.

06

Untouched Test Evaluation

Final predictive metrics are evaluated on held-out data rather than training observations.

07

Predictive / Exploratory Separation

Predictive modeling and opportunity ranking are explicitly treated as different analytical tasks.

08

Claim Discipline

The analysis does not claim causality, Google-algorithm knowledge, or guaranteed future performance.

Central Finding

The negative result is itself a research result.

Under grouped held-out validation, no tested machine-learning model improved upon the mean baseline. Ridge Regression was the strongest non-dummy model by MAE, but its MAE remained above the baseline. The appropriate interpretation is therefore limited predictive signal under the tested design—not failure of the research.

This distinction is fundamental: a rigorous empirical study should preserve evidence even when the evidence does not produce an impressive predictive score.

Measurement Validity

Target construction was independently audited.

Confirmed

Target reconstruction

The notebook reconstructs trend_pct from the relationship between impressions in the last 30 days and the previous 30 days.

26,612 rows were tested. The median absolute difference was approximately 0.022 percentage points, with 100% of audited rows matching within 0.1 percentage point.

Excluded

Leakage variables

  • impressions_last_30d
  • impressions_prev_30d
  • trend_direction
  • period-comparison click/session variables
  • 90-day performance aggregates
  • temporally entangled engagement metrics
  • content_id and client_id
  • the target itself
Methodology

From raw warehouse extract to defensible evidence.

The workflow moves sequentially from data audit to target validation, feature construction, grouped modeling, diagnostic analysis, and finally a transparent opportunity index.

01

Ingestion & Audit

Load the anonymized content warehouse extract and establish its schema, missingness, identifiers, and dimensional structure.

02

Leakage Investigation

Determine whether available fields mathematically or temporally encode the target and remove them from predictive modeling.

03

Grouped Modeling

Train Dummy, Ridge, Random Forest, and Extra Trees models under client-grouped held-out validation.

04

Decision Support

Construct a transparent opportunity score using demand, staleness, competition, and lifecycle signals.

Empirical Results

Grouped held-out validation does not support predictive superiority over the baseline.

The mean dummy regressor remains the strongest model by MAE. Ridge Regression is the best non-dummy model, but its error is still higher than the baseline.

Model MAE RMSE R² Interpretation
Dummy Regressor 65.4752 295.9562 -0.0005 Baseline
Ridge Regression 71.3596 297.5412 -0.0113 Best non-dummy model
Random Forest 88.6801 309.0226 -0.0908 Limited predictive signal
Extra Trees 88.9691 309.3702 -0.0933 Limited predictive signal


Statistical interpretation

Relative MAE improvement vs. dummy: -8.99%

Because the best non-dummy model has higher MAE than the baseline, the machine-learning system should not be presented as a validated predictive engine for future content momentum.

Decision-Support Layer

When prediction is limited, transparent prioritization can still create analytical value.

Robustness Analysis

Validation design materially changes the apparent result.

A secondary random row split was performed as a sensitivity analysis. Grouped validation remains the primary estimate because observations are nested within anonymized clients.

Validation Design MAE RMSE R²
Grouped by client 88.9691 309.3702 -0.0933
Random row split 64.7979 317.9670 -0.0128

Why this matters

Random splitting can create a more optimistic picture of generalization.

The difference demonstrates why grouped validation is retained as the primary design rather than selecting whichever split produces the most flattering metric.

Research Conclusion

What the evidence actually supports.

"The resulting system should be presented as a research-backed decision support layer for content review, not as an autonomous optimizer or a validated forecast of search performance."

The capstone establishes two principal contributions.

First: it tests whether historical content, search, competition, freshness, and production attributes contain generalizable predictive information about observed content momentum.

Second: it converts available search-intelligence context into a transparent, auditable opportunity-review system without overstating predictive or causal capability.

The study therefore prioritizes methodological defensibility over artificially impressive model performance.
